# SisFall study protocol, v1

The endpoint is a binary **recording** label. A wearable emits one evidence score per completed window. Independent event timing, prospective alarm precision and elderly fall sensitivity need additional observations.

## Cohort and validation

1. Read the native release in place. Require nine signed numeric columns and matching folder/filename identities. Fingerprint source bytes and the six retained channels. Quarantine conflicting IDs, malformed rows and exact duplicate signals. Preserve valid trials with saturated peaks or repeated measurements. The release has no per-sample clock.
2. Use waist ADXL345 and ITG3200 in release order. Map D01–D19 to ADL and F01–F15 to fall. Keep age group, activity, trial and person only as audit metadata. The clock, recording duration, padding mask, trial index and metadata are unavailable to the waveform classifiers.
3. Assign participants to five fixed groups, stratifying fall-contributing and ADL-only subjects (seed 1701). For outer group k, group (k+1) modulo five validates settings and operating points. The three remaining groups train the inner model. Refit on the four development groups, freeze the threshold/calibration, then score every trial of group k. Subject lists accompany every fold. Refit may shift score scale, so report achieved outer specificity.
4. Compare **all 22 primary models** on the same two-second, 50%-overlap representation. Additional prespecified configurations in the executable catalogue isolate representations, denoising, wavelets, geometry, fusion, pooling and model capacity/training. Comparisons with XGBoost measure preprocessing effects for that estimator. No assumption that its preferred setting transfers to a neural model.
5. Primary criterion is the mean of two quantities: mean participant sensitivity among people with falls, and mean participant specificity among people with ADLs. Select an inner operating point maximizing this criterion subject to mean specificity ≥0.95. Fit a monotone sigmoid calibrator to **validation recording maxima**. Its probability scale applies to the study's recording population. For each outer fold, select using only its inner scores. Among candidates within **0.005** of the highest eligible score prefer the smaller serialized checkpoint, then name. Report independent nested selection, fixed architecture comparisons, the best neural-only policy and ablations separately. For a final refit, apply the same rule to mean inner score and mean checkpoint size. The all-candidate, primary-only and neural-primary menus are explicit. The final all-subject model has no new independent performance estimate.

## Signal and learning

The innovation under test is to transport native-rate impact evidence and gravity-relative, co-rotating motion into a shared 20 Hz clock, then learn from trial bags. Each 50 ms token includes native magnitude extrema and absolute derivative maxima measured before filtering, even when the denoised view attenuates a peak. Body-motion features project dynamic acceleration and angular velocity onto the estimated gravity direction. The causal multilevel wavelet bank operates on **native magnitudes before tokenisation**. Static co-rotation leaves the default waveform features invariant up to numerical precision. Independently rotating sensors, changing placement and time-varying strap movement are different disturbances. Raw-axis and axis-plus-SMA controls test the information lost by invariance.

For training, sample eight evenly spaced valid windows per recording without using activity, timing annotations or signal extrema to choose their positions. Deep models optimize positive-weighted trial BCE on the maximum of their window logits. A feature-based learner uses a high-acceleration witness to initialize positive bags, then reselects witnesses with a fitted classifier and mines hard ADL negatives. This prior can miss low-impact falls. Max pooling makes repeated indices in shorter bags neutral. Validation and testing scan every valid window. No window in a positive bag receives an observed fall label. The within-window attention in DM-TCN and the maximum across windows perform different jobs.

All networks have width 12. Primary fits use AdamW, up to five epochs, batch 96 trials, gradient-norm cap 2, grouped early selection and identical seed per fold. A twelve-epoch DM-TCN checks training-budget sensitivity. The short screen compares models under a fixed budget, without establishing a fully tuned ceiling. Robust median/IQR or standard mean/SD parameters use only training tokens sampled once per second; neural inputs receive an invertible asinh compression. Classical descriptors instead receive fold-fitted feature scaling. Evaluation uses no test-fitted scalers, thresholds or calibrators.

## Controls and deployment gate

Report trial TP/FP/TN/FN, precision, sensitivity, specificity, F1, ROC-AUC, average precision, Brier score, subject uncertainty, per-activity and per-subject results. A duration-only control quantifies collection protocol leakage. An eight-window fixed-exposure analysis measures sensitivity to the number of opportunities to trigger a max. For a recording with fewer than eight distinct positions, repeat positions without changing its decision. Separately replay all ADL windows in order. Count rising alarms with a five-second refractory period and re-arm after two below-threshold windows. Reset at each recording boundary and report both total ADL hours and effective monitored exposure after warm-up. Analyze the sole older fall contributor separately.

Verify numerical invariance and stream/batch parity. Compare filtered peaks with native peaks, preserving saturations. Test frozen held-out models under synthetic sensor disturbances and label these simulations. Export the selected estimator and best neural candidate if they differ. Measure model size and host latency and validate optimized numeric outputs. Real MPU-9250 comparison, ambulatory alarm burden and physical Nano thermal/latency measurements require target recordings and hardware.
